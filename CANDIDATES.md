# Candidate lines

The judge weighs, in order: (1) one sentence, at most 200 characters, a house
cat excusing knocking a glass off the table; (2) the cat's own logic,
self-interest and total lack of remorse, never a person apologising;
(3) a fresh comic idea, NOT the obvious gravity, slippery-edge or
it-was-already-falling lines; (4) a spoken line a cat could plausibly deliver
in five seconds; (5) every character earning its place, no padding.

All ten candidates below were written for this entry. None uses gravity,
a slippery edge, or "it was already falling".

## The final (candidate 1)

> It was empty, it was in my spot, and it looked at me funny, so I handled it.

76 characters. Chosen because it packs THREE separate pieces of cat logic into
one spoken sentence -- the glass was useless (empty), it was trespassing (in my
spot), and it insulted the cat first (looked at me funny) -- and then ends on
"I handled it", the most remorseless cat euphemism available: the cat did the
job, on purpose, and considers the matter closed. No apology anywhere, no
gravity cliche, and every clause earns its characters. It reads like something
a cat would actually say in five seconds, not a paragraph of reasoning.

## The other nine

2. That glass was squatting in my sunbeam without a permit, so I evicted it. (73)
   Runner-up. Territorial cat plus deadpan bureaucracy: squatter, permit,
   eviction. Fresh and very cat, but it carries only one reason where the
   final carries three.

3. I was conducting a routine safety inspection, and that glass failed. (68)
   The cat as a smug official. Fresh and remorseless, but it leans more
   human-bureaucrat than cat, and it never says why the glass deserved it.

4. The glass was a spy for the dog, and I don't let spies live on my table. (72)
   Cat paranoia with a clear enemy (the dog). Funny, but the dog does a lot of
   the comic work and the cat's own motive is thinner.

5. That glass was keeping my water prisoner, so I set it free. (59)
   A neat reframe -- the cat as liberator -- but it is a little too cute and
   the glass itself is barely the villain.

6. It was blocking the bird, it was in my spot, and frankly it had it coming. (74)
   "Frankly it had it coming" is strong cat arrogance, and the bird is a real
   cat motive. Weaker than the final only because "blocking the bird" is a
   slightly muddier image than "looked at me funny".

7. The table is mine, the sunbeam is mine, and that glass forgot its place. (72)
   Pure territorial cat. Clean and arrogant, but "forgot its place" is a
   familiar line and the sentence explains less of the act.

8. I was testing whether the floor still works, and the glass volunteered. (71)
   "Volunteered" is a dark, funny cat word. Kept out of the final because it
   sits too close to the banned gravity-test idea.

9. That glass was staring at me, it was in my spot, and I don't tolerate witnesses. (80)
   "I don't tolerate witnesses" is the darkest punchline of the ten. It loses
   to the final because "staring at me" is weaker than "looked at me funny"
   and the ending, while funny, is less believable as a five-second excuse.

10. It was in my seat, it knew the rules, and I don't repeat myself. (64)
    Short, haughty, very cat. But it is all attitude and no reason -- the
    judge's second criterion wants the cat's logic, not just its tone.
